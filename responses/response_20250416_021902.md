# Immigration Law Query - 20250416_021902

## Query

What are the requirements for an I-601A provisional waiver? Include eligibility criteria, documentation needed, and common reasons for approval.

## Response

# I-601A Provisional Waiver Requirements

## Eligibility Criteria

To be eligible for an I-601A provisional waiver, the applicant must:

- Be the beneficiary of an approved I-130 Petition for Alien Relative or I-360 Petition for Amerasian, Widow(er), or Special Immigrant
- Have an immigrant visa case pending with the Department of State based on the approved petition
- Be physically present in the U.S. when filing the I-601A and when USCIS makes a decision on the application
- Demonstrate that refusal of admission would result in extreme hardship to a U.S. citizen or LPR spouse or parent
- Warrant a favorable exercise of discretion

Key eligibility restrictions:

- Not available if the only ground of inadmissibility is INA 212(a)(9)(C) (permanent bar)
- Not available to K visa nonimmigrants or beneficiaries of employment-based petitions
- See 8 CFR 212.7(e) for full eligibility criteria

## Required Documentation 

- Completed Form I-601A with filing fee
- Proof of an approved qualifying immigrant petition (I-130 or I-360)
- Evidence of a pending immigrant visa case and paid fees
- Evidence of extreme hardship to a qualifying relative, which may include:
  - Medical records documenting serious health conditions
  - Psychological evaluations 
  - Evidence of financial hardship (job loss, foreclosure, bankruptcy, etc.)
  - Country conditions reports related to hardship upon relocation
- Applicant's sworn statement explaining the reasons a waiver should be granted

## Common Reasons for Approval

- Qualifying relative has serious medical condition that requires applicant's care/presence
- Qualifying relative relies on applicant's income and would face financial crisis 
- Qualifying relative has significant family ties to the U.S. and can't relocate
- Dangerous country conditions in applicant's home country pose risk to qualifying relative
- Qualifying relative's age or disability makes travel difficult
- Qualifying relative is caring for applicant's children who have special needs

Note: Hardship factors are evaluated cumulatively. The more hardship factors that can be documented, the stronger the waiver application. Mere separation from family is generally not sufficient on its own.

Pitfalls to avoid:
- Filing the I-601A before the I-130 is approved or NVC case is pending 
- Failing to disclose other potential grounds of inadmissibility
- Insufficient documentation of extreme hardship

If the I-601A is approved, the applicant can depart the U.S. to attend the immigrant visa interview abroad with provisional assurance that the unlawful presence inadmissibility ground will be waived. This reduces risk compared to pursuing a traditional I-601 waiver.
